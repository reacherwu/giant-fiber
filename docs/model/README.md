# Model validation (synthetic looming stimuli)

Generated by `python3 tools/validation/validate_escape_model.py --out docs/model` with default engine
parameters. The events come from the geometric event synthesis in `giantfiber/sim/events.py`:
a 0.15 m sphere on a 320x320, 90 deg camera, 1 ms steps, 5 repeats per condition with different
noise seeds. No real event camera or fly data is used.

- `looming_validation.csv`: per trial, the angular size and time before contact at the first GF spike,
  and the escape mode.
- `looming_controls.csv`: receding, laterally translating, static and noise-only stimuli.
- `looming_validation_summary.json`: aggregates. `looming_validation.png`: membrane traces and the
  time-before-contact vs r/v fit.

What it shows (last run): the GF spike occurs at a roughly constant estimated angular size, with a mean
of 24.6 to 32.8 deg across r/v = 5 to 80 ms. Its time before contact grows linearly with r/v
(fit 2.47 * r/v + 16.0 ms, r = 0.980). Fast looms (r/v <= 20 ms) always reach short mode. r/v = 40 ms
reached short mode in 3/5 trials. r/v = 80 ms stayed in long mode in 5/5 trials. That is the
qualitative fast-short / slow-long split described by von Reyn et al. (2014). The angular thresholds
are model parameters, not fitted to electrophysiology. No control stimulus triggered an escape (0/35).
